# Figures from Translation cost

Read `references/reports/translation-cost.md` first: it says what the report counts and how to generate it. This file turns a result into the figures people ask for; the steps around it (the rates to confirm, the settings, what to say is missing) are under Figures from report data in `references/reports.md`.

**Where the numbers are (`json`).** The top level has `translationCosts`, `approvalCosts`, `totalCosts`, `savings`, `weightedUnits` and `preTranslated`, but no word totals. Words are only on the innermost member × language entries: `data[].languages[]` with groupBy=user, `data[].users[]` with groupBy=language, `data[].languages[].users[]` with groupBy=project. Each entry has `translated`, `translationCosts`, `weightedUnits` and `savings` as `{scheme: {row: value}, total}`, with the schemes `tmMatch`, `mtMatch`, `aiMatch`, `suggestionMatch` (Other translations) and `default` (No match), plus `approved` and `approvalCosts` (a `total`, and with category-based approval rates the buckets `tmMatch.perfect`, `tmMatch.100`, `mtMatch.100`, `aiMatch.100`, `suggestionMatch.100`), `preTranslated` and `totalCosts`. A scheme object such as `translated.aiMatch` holds only its rows and has no `total`: sum its rows.

**Formulas.**
- A word is a source word counted once per paid record (per target language, person, plural form and source version), not a word delivered: say so whenever words are the denominator.
- Paid words W = the sum of the entries' `translated.total`; approved words A = the sum of `approved.total`. Translation price per word = `translationCosts.total` / W; proofreading price per approved word = `approvalCosts.total` / A; all-in = `totalCosts` / W. A row's or scheme's share = its words / W, and its price = its cost / its words.
- For any slice, sum the costs and the words first and divide once.
- Where pre-translation is used, also show `totalCosts` / (W + `preTranslated.total`) as the cost per word handled, labelled approximate (a pre-translation edited later is in both volumes); it can fall while the price per paid word rises.

**Cuts.**
- *From one run.* One organization run with groupBy=project gives the organization, project, language and 'one language in these projects' figures, and workspace figures once projects are mapped to workspaces, all with one rate set; show that the parts add up to the total. One run prices every project alike, so ask whether some projects or vendors have their own rates, and run those projects separately with `projectIds`.
- *By workspace.* A workspace is a group in the API (`list_groups`, `groupId`, `generate_group_report_*`); in the answer use the user's word. Each project from `list_projects` carries its `groupId`, and `list_groups` without `parentId` lists every workspace with its `parentId`; projects at the organization's root carry the root's id, which that list leaves out. Roll nested workspaces up into their parents and show them indented: a parent's row already includes its children's. The mapping is today's: a project moved since the period counts under its current workspace, so say so.
- *By workflow.* Ask whether the user means projects grouped by workflow or the split between translation and proofreading. A project's `workflowId` is its own workflow, not a template, and Crowdin does not record which template a project came from: group projects by their step sequence (`list_workflow_steps`) and call a match to a template approximate. The Workflow step filter (project level) keeps all work on strings linked to the step, whatever step it was done at, so reports for different steps overlap and neither add up nor leave a remainder 'outside the steps'; say Crowdin does not record at which step paid work was done.
- *A release* (for example a branch in one project plus tasks in another) has no report of its own. Run one report per part with the same unit, currency, rates and options (the branch filter in a project, task mode for the tasks) and add the totals. Selected tasks that share strings in one language, such as a translation task and its proofreading task, are each priced in full, and only a branch and tasks in the same project can overlap. A deleted branch can no longer be selected: check `list_branches` first. The AI usage data has no branch, task or file: leave the AI bill out of a release total, or show an approximation separately and label it.
- *Before and after, or month by month:* one run per period with the same rates and options; a period's work spills into the next, because edits and approvals are dated by their own first save. AI started in the first month with `translated.aiMatch` or `approved.aiMatch.100` above zero; when AI runs only as pre-translation the report cannot date it, so ask.

**Proofreading against translation.** Per language, `approvalCosts` against `translationCosts`. Each approver is paid once per string, so several proofreaders on one string, not several steps, multiply the Proofreading column; a proofreader's own edits are paid as translations, not in Proofreading; and approval spend also covers pre-translations. For the value side use Translator accuracy for human work and Auto-Translation accuracy for pre-translations, with explicit Match Score Categories; the share below 100% Match is not what proofreaders changed, because another translator's approved version also lowers it. Organization reports do not show who changed a text (per project, `wasEdited` in Contribution raw data, `references/reports/contribution-raw-data.md`, marks approvals of the approver's own edit), and nothing records how serious a change was, so the verdict is the user's.

**AI in money questions.** 'AI' means this report's AI cells unless the user says they mean the AI provider's bill (`references/reports/ai-usage.md`, in USD). A sum the user quotes as spent on AI can be either, and in a finance question it is more often the bill: say which reading you use, or check which one the sum matches for the period. When an answer includes the bill, say what that period lacks (When the data exists in `references/reports/ai-usage.md`).
- *AI rows:* `translated.aiMatch` and `translationCosts.aiMatch` hold translations whose cheapest row was AI: Editor saves measured against the AI suggestions shown (row `100` = saved unchanged) and, with Use specific categories for auto-translations, edits of AI pre-translations. When that option is off (the API default), those edits are in Other translations: say AI spend and AI savings are understated.
- *AI approvals:* `approved.aiMatch.100` and `approvalCosts.aiMatch.100`, with category-based approval rates only: approvals of AI pre-translations (automatic ones too, under the account that ran them, unless Exclude approvals for edited translations is on; say so when the question is about human proofreading) and of translations whose cheapest row was AI, each at Full translation rate × the AI 100% row. The proofreading price per word of AI output is `approvalCosts.aiMatch.100` / `approved.aiMatch.100`. With flat rates it is the Proofread rate, and approvals of AI output cannot be isolated: say so.
- *AI savings:* `savings.aiMatch`, the first translation of each string in the AI rows at Full translation rate × (100% − row). AI pre-translations are not paid: their volume is inside `preTranslated`, together with TM and MT pre-translations, so that total is not a count of AI words; the savings of approved pre-translations are only in `savings.total` minus the rows of `tmMatch`, `mtMatch`, `aiMatch` and `suggestionMatch`, TM, MT and AI together: show that separately, as 'up to'.
- *100% AI price per word* = Full translation rate × the AI 100% row. It is a configured price: answer it from the rate set the user confirms without generating anything, and generate only for volumes, shares or savings. A result gives two separate figures: `translationCosts.aiMatch.100` / `translated.aiMatch.100` for AI suggestions saved unchanged in the Editor, and `approvalCosts.aiMatch.100` / `approved.aiMatch.100` for approvals of AI output, which exist only when `useCategoryBasedProofreadRates` is true.
- *AI plus proofreading price per word* depends on the route, so ask which applies or show both. AI pre-translation checked by one proofreader: the pre-translation is unpaid and each word is paid once, as an approval when approved unchanged or as an edit when changed (in the AI rows only with Use specific categories for auto-translations; the approval of one's own edit is dropped when Exclude approvals for edited translations is on), so the price = (those approval costs + those edit costs) / (those approved words + those edited words). AI suggestions used in the Editor and approved by a second person: the AI price per word (all AI rows' cost / their words) + the proofreading price per word.
- *Baseline and saving rate:* the Full translation rate (No match) is the price of a word done from scratch; for AI plus proofreading, add the proofreading price. The saving rate = 1 − the AI price per word / the Full translation rate (with custom rates, against each entry's own Full translation rate); a rate against the Full translation rate alone covers the translation part only, so label it. The change of the overall price per word against months or a language without AI is a separate figure that mixes in TM and the rest of the match mix, not the AI saving rate.
- *What AI saved* in money is `savings.aiMatch`, the Savings cell of the AI rows (first translations only, at the request's rates). Never compute it as Full translation rate × AI words − AI cost, which also counts later translators. `savings.total` also holds Other translations and approved pre-translations, so the TM, MT and AI rows do not add up to it. 'Spend on AI' in this report is the AI rows plus the AI approval bucket; set savings against the AI bill only in one currency, with an exchange rate from the user.
- *Quality* is not in this report: Auto-Translation accuracy per AI prompt for pre-translations and Translator accuracy for Editor work, with explicit `matchScoreCategories`; both measure how much was changed before approval, not errors.
- Take savings from this report, not from Translation Savings, which splits them another way (`references/reports/translation-savings.md`).
